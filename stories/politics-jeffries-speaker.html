House Democrats Nominate Hakeem Jeffries for Speaker, Putting Him on Track to Be the First Black Speaker

Jeffries won the closed-door caucus vote 251-11. The full House will elect its speaker on Jan. 3, when Democrats take the majority.

7:30 PM EST November 18, 2026 · 2 min read · By Bram Basow

House Democrats on Wednesday nominated Rep. Hakeem Jeffries of New York to be the next speaker of the House, setting him on course to become the first Black lawmaker to hold the gavel when Democrats take control of the chamber in January.

Jeffries, who ran unopposed, won the nomination 251-11 in a closed-door vote of current and incoming Democratic members. He has led House Democrats as minority leader since 2023.

The full House will elect its speaker on Jan. 3, when the new Congress convenes. Democrats are set to hold at least 257 seats, with several races still uncalled, well above the 218 votes needed to win the speakership.

Jeffries asked for a recorded vote rather than a vote by acclamation, saying it was the approach Democrats took in 2018, the last time they moved from the minority to the majority.

A focus on the cost of living

After the vote, Jeffries said House Democrats would fight to lower the cost of groceries, gas and housing and to make health care affordable.

Asked about the members who voted against him, he said: "The results of today's election speak for themselves. We're going to work hard throughout every corner of the caucus to deliver for the American people." He added, "Our job is to fight MAGA extremism, not each other."

Progressive holdouts

Several of the no votes came from incoming members on the party's left, some of whom won with the backing of the Democratic Socialists of America and have pushed for votes on Medicare for All and on limiting U.S. military aid to Israel.

Seven incoming members issued a joint statement after the vote explaining their opposition: Darializa Avila Chevalier and Claire Valdez of New York, Adam Hamawy of New Jersey, Frederick Haynes of Texas, Melat Kiros of Colorado, Donavan McKinney of Michigan and Chris Rabb of Pennsylvania. "While we have seen meaningful progress, there is still work to be done to ensure that the direction our party takes this coming Congress reflects what our constituents are demanding of leadership," they said.

No other Democrat challenged Jeffries for the job, and the size of the incoming majority gives him room to absorb some dissent.

The rest of the leadership team

Democrats also chose Rep. Katherine Clark of Massachusetts as majority leader, making her the first woman to hold that post, Rep. Pete Aguilar of California as majority whip and Rep. Joe Neguse of Colorado as assistant speaker. Rep. Ted Lieu of California was elected caucus chair.

House Republicans picked Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio last week to serve as minority leader in the next Congress. In the Senate, Democratic leader Chuck Schumer of New York faces a challenge from Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, with Senate leadership elections set for early December.
