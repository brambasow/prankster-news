Your Pay Compared to the CEO's of Top US Companies

The typical S&P 500 chief executive made $17.7 million last year, about what the median worker would earn in 200 years.

October 19, 2026 · 4 min read · Summary by Prankster News
Based on reporting by the Associated Press and data from Equilar

According to a new Associated Press survey of S&P 500 companies, the typical CEO compensation package rose nearly 6% in 2025 to $17.7 million, while the median employee earned about $90,000. At half the companies surveyed, it would take the median worker 200 years to earn what their CEO made in just one year.

The survey, conducted with data from Equilar, examined pay packages for 337 executives who had served at least two full consecutive years. It included only companies that filed proxy statements between January 1 and April 30, 2026, to avoid distortions from one-time sign-on bonuses.

Stock awards make up the largest share of most packages. While base salaries and cash bonuses rose modestly, the biggest jumps came from equity grants tied to company performance and stock price. Some of the largest packages included massive one-time awards. Welltower CEO Shankh Mitra’s compensation was valued at roughly $821 million. Other extreme ratios appeared at companies such as Coca-Cola (CEO earned about 1,739 times the median worker) and TJX (about 1,774 times).

The median employee pay across the surveyed companies was approximately $89,744–$90,000. At half the firms, the CEO-to-median-worker ratio meant a typical employee would need 200 years to match the CEO’s single-year compensation — up from 192 years in the previous survey.

These gaps have drawn attention from advocates and some local governments. Campaigns in San Francisco and Los Angeles have pushed for higher taxes on companies with especially large CEO-to-worker pay ratios. Companies have been required to disclose these ratios since 2018.
