Tech Leaders and Trump Back a Push to Reinstate Jensen Huang, but Nvidia's Board Isn't Budging

A petition to bring back Nvidia's ousted co-founder has drawn support from Elon Musk, other tech executives and President Trump, but the board is not considering his return.

1:55 AM EST November 17, 2026 · 3 min read · by Prankster News Staff

A campaign to return Jensen Huang to the top job at Nvidia picked up momentum Monday, drawing support from some of the technology industry's best-known executives and from President Donald Trump. Nvidia's board, however, is not considering bringing him back, according to people familiar with its thinking cited by the BBC, CBS News, The Hill and The Washington Post.

Huang resigned last Thursday, ending a 33-year run at the chipmaker he co-founded, after revelations of extramarital affairs. One was with a woman U.S. intelligence agencies suspect of spying for China, and another was with a senior Nvidia executive who reported to him. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent replacement.

The petition

The "Stand with Jensen" petition, set up last week, asks the board to bring Huang back while an independent investigation proceeds. By Monday it had drawn roughly 175,000 to 180,000 signatures, according to tallies reported by the BBC, CNBC and CBS News. "We are concerned that Jensen was removed without meaningful due process," its organizers wrote in an open letter.

Signers include Tesla and SpaceX chief Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, Shopify CEO Tobias Lütke and Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick. "A petition is underway to reinstate Jensen and I'm signing it too," Musk posted on X. Sweeney wrote, "Save Jensen!" The petition's website notes that signers' affiliations are self-reported and not verified, and the Associated Press said the identities of the signatories could not be independently verified.

Two major shareholders have also weighed in. Brad Gerstner of Altimeter Capital and Gavin Baker of Atreides Management urged the board in an open letter to leave "all options on the table, including the potential return of Jensen Huang."

Huang himself has discussed a comeback with confidants, the Financial Times reported. One ally told the paper that supporters were assembling a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to lobby for his return and would seek the White House's backing.

Trump weighs in

Speaking to reporters Monday as he left the White House for a trip to Asia, Trump said he would welcome Huang's return. "I think he learned a big lesson," Trump said, adding that he "would certainly not mind" seeing Huang reinstated. "There's a big group of people that would like him to return," he said.

Not everyone in the administration agrees. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Sunday that Nvidia should hire its next chief executive from outside the company, amid questions about whether Nvidia chips sold to companies in Singapore and elsewhere in Asia ended up in China despite U.S. export controls.

The board and Wall Street

Board members are not interested in reinstating Huang as CEO or in any other role, a source directly familiar with their thinking told CBS News, and the BBC and The Hill reported that the board is not weighing his return. Wall Street analysts are skeptical too. "We see no chance Jensen comes back to Nvidia as CEO," Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives said in a note to investors, adding that the best path forward for the company is a new external CEO or Kress.

The scandal has also drawn Washington's attention. Huang has been summoned to testify before Congress about whether the affair compromised national security. On Monday, the Commerce Department announced new rules requiring companies in Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia and Brazil to obtain U.S. export licenses for the most advanced chips, an effort to stop Chinese firms from reaching them through overseas data centers. China has denied that the woman, Alice Huang, who is not related to Jensen Huang, is a spy. Megaspeed, the Singapore company where she was an executive, says she left the business in 2025.

Nvidia's shares have fallen sharply since the scandal broke, erasing hundreds of billions of dollars in market value. Kress is scheduled to present the company's quarterly results on Tuesday, when investors will be listening for word on Nvidia's leadership and outlook.
