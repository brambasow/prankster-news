Georgia Reclaims No. 1 as Playoff Picture Sharpens Amid Ohio State’s Collapse

Georgia is back at No. 1 after a 42-7 win at Ole Miss, while Ohio State has dropped to three losses.

10:22 PM EST November 8, 2026 · 4 min read · by Prankster News Staff

With the first College Football Playoff rankings already released and Week 10 in the books, the race toward the 12-team championship has begun to clarify. Georgia has returned to the top of the rankings, Ohio State’s season has unraveled, and a cluster of undefeated and one-loss teams are positioning themselves for December.

Georgia back at No. 1

The Georgia Bulldogs (8-1) reclaimed the No. 1 spot in both the AP Top 25 and Coaches Poll after a dominant 42-7 victory at Ole Miss on Saturday. The performance was one of the most complete of the season for Kirby Smart’s team and came on the same weekend that previous No. 1 Miami fell 31-24 at Notre Dame. Georgia received most first-place votes (53 of 70 in the AP poll).

It is the second time this season the Bulldogs have held the top ranking. They briefly sat at No. 1 earlier before an upset loss to Auburn, then climbed back through the top five. With a remaining schedule that includes Missouri and a road trip to South Carolina, Georgia is in strong position to contend for an SEC title and a top playoff seed. The win over Ole Miss also snapped a long home winning streak for the Rebels and reinforced Georgia’s defensive and overall physical identity.

Ohio State’s free fall

While Georgia has risen, Ohio State has plummeted. The Buckeyes are 6-3 after a 41-28 home loss to Oregon, their second straight defeat (following a loss to USC) and their third of the season. It is the first time since 2011 that Ohio State has had three regular-season losses by early November. The team has dropped to No. 21 in the AP poll and No. 20 in the Coaches Poll—its lowest ranking in more than a decade.

The injury to starting quarterback Julian Sayin has been a major factor. Without him, the offense has lacked its previous explosiveness, and the defense has surrendered large stretches of points. Ryan Day’s program entered the year with national title expectations as the preseason No. 1; those hopes are effectively gone. Playoff odds have collapsed, and the remaining games against Northwestern, Nebraska, and Michigan now carry more of a salvage tone than a championship one. The collapse has drawn sharp scrutiny in Columbus, where three losses this early are rare.

Teams most likely to reach the playoff

Projections after Week 10 point to a field dominated by a handful of clear contenders. Georgia sits near the top of virtually every model. Undefeated Utah (9-0) is right behind after a solid win over BYU and holds its highest ranking in program history. Notre Dame (8-1) strengthened its case with the win over Miami. UCLA (9-0) remains the only other unbeaten Power conference team and has climbed into the top four. Indiana (8-1) and Miami (8-1) also sit firmly in the conversation.

Conference representation

The SEC currently holds the deepest group of viable contenders. Georgia, Alabama, Texas, and Tennessee all appear in many projected brackets, and additional two- or three-loss SEC teams could still factor in depending on how the final weeks unfold. Most models suggest the SEC will send three to five teams to the 12-team field.

The Big Ten is more top-heavy: UCLA and Indiana are the strongest bets, with the rest of the conference (including the fading Ohio State) needing significant help. The Big 12 is led by undefeated Utah, with Texas Tech and possibly BYU or others in the conversation. The ACC’s best chance runs through Miami, with Wake Forest as a potential second entry. One Group of 5 team is expected to claim the automatic spot, currently projected as Boise State.

With several undefeated and one-loss teams still alive and the official CFP rankings set to update, the next few weekends will determine which of these contenders lock in their places and which conferences ultimately send the most teams to the expanded playoff. Georgia’s return to No. 1 has set a clear standard at the top; the rest of the field is still sorting itself out beneath them.

Sources: AP Top 25, USA TODAY Coaches Poll, Football Foundation (FWAA-NFF Super 16), ESPN, FOX Sports, CBS Sports, USA TODAY, Eleven Warriors, Sports Illustrated, GordStats, AJC.
