Democrat Mary Peltola Unseats Sen. Dan Sullivan in Alaska, Giving Democrats a 56-44 Senate Majority

Peltola cleared 50% of the first-choice vote, so no ranked-choice count was needed. The former congresswoman will be the first Alaska Native in the Senate, and hers was the last Senate race of the midterms to be decided.

1:45 PM EST November 18, 2026 · 4 min read · By Bram Basow

Democrat Mary Peltola has defeated Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan in Alaska's U.S. Senate race, flipping the seat and giving Democrats a 56-44 majority in the Senate. The Associated Press, NBC News and CBS News called the race for Peltola on Tuesday night, two weeks after the Nov. 3 election.

Alaska was the last Senate contest of the midterms to be resolved. Democrats flipped nine Republican-held seats this year, enough to retake the chamber, and also won control of the House.

With about 99% of ballots counted, Peltola had 50.1% of the first-choice vote to Sullivan's 45.8%, a lead of 12,458 votes out of 287,896 counted. Republican Gerald Heikes had 2.1%, and Dan J. Sullivan, a retired teacher who shares the senator's name, had 1.7%. The results remain unofficial until the state certifies the election.

No ranked-choice count needed

Under Alaska's system, the top four finishers in an all-party primary advance to the general election, and voters rank the candidates in order of preference. If no one wins more than half of the first-choice votes, the last-place candidate is eliminated and those voters' next choices are counted, a process that repeats until someone has a majority.

Because Peltola cleared 50% of the first-choice vote, the race was decided without a ranked-choice tabulation. Ballot data released by state election officials had indicated she would also have won if a ranked-choice count had been needed, according to the AP and the Anchorage Daily News.

Before the race was called, Sullivan's campaign said it would not comment on individual ballot updates or outside projections. "Alaskans deserve a complete and accurate count, and we will let that process run its course until every legal vote is counted and certified," campaign spokesperson Nate Adams said in a statement Monday.

A political comeback

Peltola, 53, won Alaska's at-large House seat in a 2022 special election after the death of longtime Republican Rep. Don Young, becoming the first Alaska Native elected to Congress, and won a full term that November. She lost her 2024 reelection bid to Republican Nick Begich by less than 3 percentage points, in a year when President Donald Trump carried Alaska by 13 points.

Peltola, who is Yup'ik, will be the first Alaska Native to serve in the Senate. She is also the first Democrat elected to the Senate from Alaska since 2008. Sullivan, a two-term senator, had been seeking a third term.

"From Juneau to Utqiagvik, the message from Alaskans is clear: the status quo in DC is not working for Alaska," Peltola said in a statement Tuesday night. "As a lifelong Alaskan who has dedicated my life to serving our state, there is no greater honor than having Alaskans' support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs, and put Alaska first."

Peltola presented herself as an independent voice for the state and campaigned against Sullivan on high fuel and diesel prices and the war with Iran, issues that weighed on Republican candidates this year.

A race marked by controversy

The contest was shadowed by the candidacy of Dan J. Sullivan, a political newcomer. The New York Times reported that members of Peltola's campaign were involved in recruiting him, and Republicans argued that a second Dan Sullivan on the ballot could confuse voters and pull support from the incumbent. Peltola denied involvement. State courts allowed him to remain on the ballot.

Trump called for Peltola to be prosecuted, and the Justice Department opened an investigation. Republican Gov. Mike Dunleavy ordered a separate state investigation. The Justice Department also sued to try to stop Alaska from certifying the result if Sullivan lost, citing possible voter confusion over the two names. A federal judge dismissed that effort.

In the end, the dispute did not decide the race. CBS News and NBC News reported that Peltola's margin was large enough that confusion over the two Sullivans did not affect the outcome, and a BBC Verify analysis found she would have won even if every vote for Dan J. Sullivan had gone to the incumbent.

Peltola also faced allegations from former staff members about her treatment of aides during the campaign. Her campaign said the accounts were exaggerated.
