Trump Names Stephen Miller White House Chief of Staff, Replacing Susie Wiles After Midterm Losses

Trump named Stephen Miller chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles, and said the midterms were not the reason.

November 6, 2026 · 4 min read · by Prankster News Staff

President Donald Trump announced Friday that Stephen Miller will replace Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff, effective Monday. The change comes days after Republicans lost control of both the House and the Senate in the midterm elections. Trump publicly denied that the election results prompted the move.

“Not because of the election. It’s just time,” Trump told reporters as he left the White House. “Sometimes you change. The election wasn’t my election. I wasn’t on the ballot.” He praised Miller as a longtime loyalist who has been with him “from the beginning” and described him as “a good manager.” In a Truth Social post, Trump called Miller “one of my most trusted advisors” and said Wiles had done “an outstanding job” and would move to the Trump political operation.

Wiles, the first woman to serve as White House chief of staff, submitted her resignation Thursday night. In a statement, she said serving in the White House had been “the honor of a lifetime” and pointed to achievements on the border, taxes, and the economy. She added that while her role was changing, her commitment to the president and the mission would not.

Miller’s role and background

Miller, 41, has served as deputy chief of staff for policy and homeland security adviser in Trump’s second term. He has been a principal architect of the administration’s immigration agenda and has wielded significant influence on domestic policy. He first joined Trump’s orbit as a speechwriter on the 2016 campaign and has remained one of the president’s most consistently hardline advisers across both terms.

Where Wiles was often viewed as a manager who sought to brief the president on risks and maintain operational discipline, Miller has been described by associates as more of an accelerant for Trump’s policy instincts. With Democrats set to control both chambers of Congress for the remainder of Trump’s term, the White House is expected to lean more heavily on executive authority. Miller’s elevation is widely seen as a signal that the administration intends to press forward on its core priorities rather than moderate them in response to the election results.

The political backdrop

Multiple outlets, citing sources familiar with internal discussions, reported that Trump was angry over the scale of the midterm losses and had directed some of that frustration at Wiles. Publicly, however, the president framed the decision as routine. Wiles had previously indicated she planned to remain through the lame-duck session or into early next year; the timeline was accelerated.

The appointment also draws attention to immigration policy, which Miller has driven and which proved politically costly for Republicans in the midterms. Exit polling indicated that a majority of voters felt the administration’s enforcement actions had gone too far. Elevating the principal author of those policies to the top staff job underscores that Trump is not retreating from them.

Miller takes office on Monday as the 33rd White House chief of staff. How the new arrangement functions with a Democratic Congress will ultimately shape the final two years of Trump’s presidency, whether for good or bad, only time will tell.

Sources: White House statements, Truth Social posts, CNN, Reuters, CBS News, BBC, Axios, The Atlantic, New York Times.
