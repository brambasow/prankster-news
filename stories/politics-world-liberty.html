World Liberty, UAE Deals, and the President’s Family

A shareholder lawsuit raises the appearance problem of foreign-linked crypto deals at a company the president’s family co-founded.

8:04 PM EDT October 27, 2026 · 5 min read · by Prankster News Staff

A Politico report describes a shareholder lawsuit that cuts to a basic problem of appearance: a sitting president is alleged to be receiving large sums of money tied to foreign government linked deals involving a crypto company his family co-founded. Even if Donald Trump is not personally running World Liberty Financial day to day, the fact that his sons are directly involved makes the arrangement his problem. Family businesses do not create a clean separation when the president is the one whose office supplies the value.

According to the complaint first reported by Politico, the first deal involved the sale of a 49 percent stake in World Liberty Financial for $500 million “routed from” the UAE before Trump’s second term began. The suit claims Trump has received millions in payouts from that transaction since taking office. A World Liberty spokesperson told Politico that Trump was not involved in the transaction and currently has no role with the company. That defense is noted, but it does not erase the structure. The company was co-founded by Trump and his sons. When the president’s children are principals in an enterprise that is taking hundreds of millions from a foreign source, and when money continues to flow to the family after the inauguration, the president is not a bystander. The public reasonably sees the family as a unit.

The second deal, as described in the Politico account, involves a UAE government-created investment fund purchasing $2 billion of the Trump family’s digital currency. The lawsuit calls this a scheme “to inflate the value of President Trump’s crypto holdings” and says it handed him millions in interest while expanding the circulation of his stablecoin. Again, the company and the White House reject the claims. White House spokesperson Anna Kelly called the suit “frivolous” and said there are “no conflicts of interest,” adding that Trump “only acts in the best interests of the American public.” Those are the official responses. They do not change the underlying picture of a foreign entity directing large sums toward a venture whose success is tied to the sitting president’s name and office.

The Foreign Emoluments Clause exists precisely because the founders worried about foreign governments using money to influence American officials. The lawsuit invokes that clause and argues that “no president in American history has profited from his office so brazenly.” Whether a court ultimately agrees is a legal question that has not been decided. What is already clear from the facts reported in the Politico piece is the optics. A president does not need to sit in the CEO’s chair for the arrangement to look like he is personally benefiting from foreign money while in office. When his children run or own the business, conflicts of interest do arise.

The plaintiff is seeking to have the court strip Trump of the payments, force disclosure of any other foreign crypto payments, and award damages. Circle, the plaintiff’s former employer, has distanced itself from the lawsuit, saying it did not authorize the filing. But that procedural detail does not diminish the central issue raised by the reporting: large sums tied to a Middle Eastern government allegedly flowed to a business co-founded by the president’s family, with payouts continuing after he took office. That is a troubling appearance for any president, regardless of formal titles or corporate disclaimers. His children’s involvement does not insulate him, it further reinforces the connection. The key question is what the United Arab Emirates (UAE) received in return for its payments to the Trump family business. That is the billion-dollar question.
