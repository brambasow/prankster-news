The War in Gaza: Death Tolls, Humanitarian Conditions, the Genocide Question, and U.S. Support

The Gaza Health Ministry reports roughly 74,645 Palestinians killed as of mid-November 2026.

9:33 PM EST November 10, 2026 · 6 min read · by Prankster News Staff

The war that began with Hamas’s attack on Israel on October 7, 2023, has produced one of the highest-casualty conflicts of the 21st century. As of mid-November 2026, the Gaza Health Ministry reports roughly 74,645 Palestinians killed and nearly 176,000 injured since the start of the fighting. Israeli authorities report approximately 2,347 Israelis and foreign nationals killed over the same period (including about 1,200 on October 7 itself and more than 1,300 security personnel since). A ceasefire took effect in October 2025, but sporadic Israeli strikes and other violence have continued, and living conditions for most of Gaza’s roughly 2.1 million people remain dire.

Casualty Figures and Their Sources

The most widely cited Palestinian death toll comes from the Gaza Ministry of Health, which operates under Hamas administration. As of November 10, 2026, it listed 74,645 killed (including roughly 22,000 children and 12,500 women according to some breakdowns) and about 176,000 injured. These figures are compiled from hospital records, morgue data, media reports, and family submissions; they do not distinguish combatants from civilians. Many bodies remain unrecovered under rubble.

Israeli authorities and some independent analysts have disputed the precision and methodology of these numbers, pointing to incomplete identification, possible inclusion of natural deaths or combatants counted as civilians, and earlier discrepancies between different Hamas-linked reporting offices. At the same time, several studies (including analyses published in medical journals and reviews by groups such as Airwars) have found the ministry’s named lists to be broadly consistent with available cross-checks, and some Israeli military assessments have treated the overall scale as roughly accurate even while rejecting the civilian-combatant breakdown. The United Nations and most major news organizations continue to cite the ministry figures with these caveats.

On the Israeli side, Defense Ministry data as of early October 2026 recorded 1,318 security personnel killed across all fronts since October 7, 2023, plus roughly 1,029 civilians killed in attacks (including 63 children). The October 7 assault itself killed about 1,200 people in Israel, the large majority civilians, and involved the taking of hostages.

Current Living Conditions and the Humanitarian Crisis

Nearly the entire population of Gaza has been displaced at least once. Assessments from the UN Shelter Cluster and others indicate that roughly 94 percent of the population lacks adequate shelter. Large numbers live in tents or makeshift structures; recent heavy rains in early November 2026 flooded tens of thousands of tents and damaged or destroyed many more. Damaged buildings that still house people remain at risk of collapse.

Infrastructure destruction is extensive: large majorities of housing units, schools, and water/sanitation systems have been damaged or destroyed. Most hospitals are only partially functional or closed. Food insecurity remains severe; Integrated Food Security Phase Classification analyses have repeatedly placed large shares of the population in Crisis or Emergency categories. Overcrowding, poor sanitation, and limited clean water have driven rises in communicable disease. Aid entry continues under restrictions, and reconstruction has been minimal. A Palestinian technocratic committee was established under the ceasefire framework, but Israeli control of significant portions of the territory (around half or more under some accounts) and restrictions on materials have limited recovery.

The Genocide Question

South Africa brought a case against Israel at the International Court of Justice under the Genocide Convention. In early 2024 the ICJ found a plausible risk to the rights of Palestinians under the Convention and ordered provisional measures requiring Israel to prevent genocidal acts, punish incitement, and enable humanitarian aid. It did not issue a final determination on the merits. The written pleadings phase continues, with further submissions scheduled into 2027 and 2029.

An independent UN Commission of Inquiry has concluded that Israel has committed genocide in Gaza (including continued acts after the 2025 ceasefire), citing patterns of killing, serious harm, conditions of life calculated to destroy the group in part, and statements by officials as evidence of intent. Some human-rights organizations have reached similar conclusions. Israel rejects the characterization as false and politically motivated, describing its operations as legitimate self-defense against Hamas (an organization that has itself been accused of war crimes, including the deliberate killing and hostage-taking of civilians on October 7). The United States and several other governments have also rejected the genocide allegation. Establishing genocide under international law requires proof of specific intent to destroy the protected group in whole or in part—a high threshold that remains contested in the courts and in public debate.

U.S. Military Assistance

The United States has been Israel’s primary military supplier for decades. A 2016 Memorandum of Understanding committed roughly $3.8 billion annually in military financing (mostly Foreign Military Financing for U.S. weapons purchases) through fiscal year 2028. Since October 2023, additional wartime aid and arms transfers have pushed the total well above $20 billion according to multiple analyses (including Brown University’s Costs of War project). Weapons delivered or approved have included bombs, missiles, aircraft, and other munitions used in the Gaza campaign.

In late 2026, President Trump stated that U.S. military aid to Israel would end by year’s end. Subsequent reporting indicated ongoing negotiations for a shorter, five-year package that would maintain annual levels near current amounts before phasing out direct financing, while possibly increasing joint air- and missile-defense funding. Most of the aid is spent on American-made systems, supporting U.S. defense industry production lines. Critics argue the assistance has enabled high civilian casualties and infrastructure destruction; supporters frame it as necessary for a key ally facing multiple threats, including from Hamas, Hezbollah, and Iran.

The conflict has produced enormous human costs on both sides, destroyed large parts of Gaza’s built environment, and left unresolved legal and political questions that will continue to be litigated in international forums and debated in policy circles for years. Casualty totals, the precise civilian-combatant ratio, and the legal characterization of Israel’s conduct remain subjects of active dispute among governments, courts, and analysts.

Their intentional suppression of a group of people forcing them to live in conditions that most Americans would consider unhealthy or inhumane, what do we call that in the English dictionary?
