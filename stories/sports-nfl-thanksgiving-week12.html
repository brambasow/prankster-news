NFL Week 12 Matchups

The Bears, Eagles and Bills each won late on Thursday. Black Friday starts a weekend that includes a 49ers-Seahawks showdown and a Monday night race in the NFC South.

12:15 AM EST November 27, 2026 · 4 min read · By Bram Basow

Thanksgiving week in the NFL delivered three games that were settled in the last moments. Chicago, Philadelphia and Buffalo each came away with a late winner on Thursday, and the results reshuffled the NFC North, badly hurt Dallas's playoff hopes and gave Buffalo more breathing room at the top of the AFC. The league opened the week on Wednesday night with its first Thanksgiving Eve game, a 26-13 win by the Los Angeles Rams over the Green Bay Packers.

Bears 39, Lions 35

Detroit led for nearly the entire game, and running back Jahmyr Gibbs carried 21 times for 205 yards and three touchdowns. Chicago still came back from an 11-point hole in the fourth quarter. Caleb Williams won it with a 6-yard throw to Rome Odunze on third-and-goal, with three seconds on the clock. Odunze kept his feet in bounds at the back of the end zone. Williams finished 30 of 44 for 345 yards and two touchdowns. The Bears (9-2) now sit a game ahead of the Lions (8-3) in the NFC North. Lions coach Dan Campbell said the loss "should sting for a while" before the team turns the page.

Eagles 39, Cowboys 38

Dallas built a 31-10 lead at home, and then the game flipped. Philadelphia was without starting quarterback Jalen Hurts, and Joe Milton, who once played for the Cowboys, threw for 331 yards and three touchdowns and added a rushing score. DeVonta Smith caught 11 passes for 200 yards and two touchdowns. A blocked punt that the Eagles returned for a touchdown early in the fourth quarter trimmed the margin to seven. Late in the game, Dallas led by two and coach Brian Schottenheimer chose to go for it on fourth-and-1 from the Philadelphia 39. Dak Prescott was sacked for a nine-yard loss, and Jake Elliott then kicked a 59-yard field goal with two seconds left. It was the biggest comeback in Thanksgiving Day history. Dallas fell to 5-7, and Next Gen Stats now gives the Cowboys an 8% chance of making the playoffs. The Eagles are 4-7.

Bills 13, Chiefs 10

Rain, wind and cold in Orchard Park kept both offenses in check, and neither Josh Allen nor Patrick Mahomes threw a touchdown pass. Allen was 10 of 21 for 81 yards with two interceptions, and Mahomes was 16 of 29 for 174 yards. James Cook ran for 117 yards and a 5-yard score for Buffalo. Tyler Bass tied the game with a 54-yard field goal in the fourth quarter, and Kansas City kicker Harrison Butker later missed from 46 yards. On the Chiefs' last punt, Buffalo's special teams tipped the kick, Greg Dortch returned it to the Kansas City 39, and Bass connected from 57 yards as time ran out. The Bills (10-1) have the AFC's best record. The Chiefs (8-3) are half a game behind Denver in the AFC West.

Black Friday: Broncos at Steelers

Denver (8-2) goes to Pittsburgh (6-4) on Friday at 3 p.m. ET on Prime Video, looking for a seventh straight win. A victory would put the Broncos a full game ahead of Kansas City in the division. The Steelers have a new look at quarterback. They announced Tuesday night that Mason Rudolph would start and Aaron Rodgers would be inactive, and they released Rodgers on Wednesday after he criticized coach Mike McCarthy on "The Pat McAfee Show." Pittsburgh is 4-0 at home this season.

Sunday, Nov. 29

The 4:25 p.m. ET game between the Seahawks and the 49ers carries the most weight. San Francisco (9-1) leads the NFC West by two games over Seattle (6-4) and has already beaten the Seahawks this season, so Seattle has little room left to slip. The Bears are only a half-game behind the 49ers at 9-2, which adds interest to the race for the NFC's top seed. Sunday night, the Browns (6-4) host the Raiders (4-7) on NBC. Cleveland holds the AFC's sixth seed and is coming off a bye, and Las Vegas has rookie quarterback Fernando Mendoza, the No. 1 overall pick, trying to keep a faint playoff chance alive.

Monday, Nov. 30

The week ends with Carolina at Tampa Bay (8:15 p.m. ET, ESPN) in the NFC South. The Panthers (5-5) lead the division by two games over the Buccaneers (3-7), Falcons and Saints. A Tampa Bay win would cut that gap to one game.
