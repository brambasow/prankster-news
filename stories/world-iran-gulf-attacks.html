Iran Targets U.S. Air Base in Kuwait and Sites in Bahrain as Tit-for-Tat Strikes Return to the Gulf

Kuwait and Bahrain said their air defenses intercepted the attacks, which came after U.S. aircraft struck an Iranian missile site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz.

7:30 PM EST November 18, 2026 · 2 min read · By Bram Basow

Iran targeted a U.S. air base in Kuwait overnight and launched several attacks on Bahrain, hours after American forces struck an Iranian missile launch site in the Strait of Hormuz. The strikes mark a return of tit-for-tat attacks in the Persian Gulf after a quiet period of roughly two months between the two countries.

Iran's army said its drones "targeted Patriot air defence systems and drone and missile launch platforms" at Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait, which hosts U.S. forces, according to a statement carried by Iranian state television. It was the first Iranian attack on Kuwait in more than two months.

Kuwait's military said its air defenses intercepted the drones, and it did not report any damage. The Kuwaiti Foreign Ministry condemned the attack, saying it threatened the country's security and stability.

Bahrain's military said its air defenses intercepted several Iranian attacks early Wednesday. The small island kingdom hosts the U.S. Navy's 5th Fleet.

A U.S. strike on Qeshm Island

The Iranian attacks followed a U.S. strike on Tuesday night, when American aircraft hit a missile launch site belonging to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz. A U.S. military official said the strike was a response to "IRGC aggression targeting U.S. personnel and assets." Iranian state media quoted a local official as saying the strike caused no damage to infrastructure on the island.

Iran has repeatedly targeted U.S. facilities in Bahrain over the past week, including radar installations on Friday, Sunday and Tuesday. U.S. officials have said those attacks were intercepted. Iran has said its recent strikes were a response to U.S. drone surveillance flights and an attack on Iranian communications towers.

U.S. Central Command said it remains "highly vigilant and fully postured to protect Americans across the region."

Nine months of war

The war began on Feb. 28, when the United States and Israel attacked Iran. Since then, Iran has fired missiles and drones at Israel and at Gulf Arab states that host U.S. forces, and has attacked commercial ships in and around the Strait of Hormuz.

The United States has kept a naval blockade on Iranian ports since the summer, while U.S. military escorts have helped oil tankers resume passage through the strait. Iran has continued to strike ships in the area. The Abu Dhabi National Oil Company said Wednesday that a crew member was missing after one of its tankers was attacked in the strait on Tuesday, and that 11 crew members were receiving medical care.

Talks on a diplomatic end to the war, mediated in part by Qatar, have stalled, and the renewed exchanges of fire have raised fears of a wider escalation.
